I have been writing software for more than thirty years and I am still at it, which probably
tells you how I feel about it. Juniper, then Red Hat, on open source networking -- Open vSwitch
and OVN, the plumbing under OpenStack and Kubernetes. Over time it became clear that
[the people are even better than the work](post/main/engineer-as-a-service/).

I had expected retirement to feel like an ending. Instead the calendar got quieter and the days
got fuller, and most of what fills them has nothing to do with a keyboard.

My old friends and my family come first -- they are very dear to me, and we are all aging
together now. That is not a line for a page like this; it is simply where the week goes. The
rest of this page is the other things.

    <h2>Gleaning</h2>
    <p>
      A few mornings a month I drive out to a field that has already been harvested and walk it
      with a dozen other people, picking up what got left behind. Sweet potatoes, squash,
      cabbage, apples. On a good day it is thousands of pounds before lunch, and it reaches food
      pantries and meal programs around eastern Massachusetts within a few days.
    </p>
    <p>
      Nobody out there needs me to be clever. You harvest, you box, and you make new friends.
      What is not to love about that? If you are
      anywhere near Boston and you have a free morning, the
      <a href="https://www.bostonareagleaners.org/">Boston Area Gleaners</a> will gladly put you
      to work.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

  <div class="about-card-text">
    <h2>Sitting still</h2>
    <p>
      Now and then I sit a ten-day Vipassana course at
      <a href="https://dhara.dhamma.org/">Dhamma Dhar&#257;</a>, up in Shelburne. Sometimes I
      serve one instead, helping with the maintenance of the place. Sitting means no phone, no
      reading, no writing, no talking. It is the hardest thing I do, and I keep going back.
    </p>
    <p>
      I will not try to explain the technique here; anyone who sums it up in a paragraph is
      doing it a disservice, me included. What I will say is that the courses are free &ndash; not
      subsidized, not discounted, free. They run entirely on donations from people who finished a
      course and wanted the next person to have the same chance. That still gets me, every time.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

  <div class="about-card-text">
    <h2>Volunteering at QCon</h2>
    <p>
      I hold up the five-minute card at the back of the room, run a microphone down an aisle, and
      tell people where track three is. It is the best seat in the house and you earn it by being
      useful. Most recently
      <a href="https://boston.qcon.ai/">QCon AI in Boston</a>, and London before that.
    </p>
    <p>
      What I did not see coming is that the crew would be the best part. Smart, generous people
      who show up early and stay late for no reason other than wanting the event to succeed.
      That is why I keep signing up.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

  <div class="about-card-text">
    <h2>Still building things</h2>
    <p>
      The soldering iron did not retire. There is
      <a href="post/hacks/office-clock-part3/">a clock on my office wall</a> I have been
      rebuilding every few years since 2016, another one beside the bed, a pile of small things
      that gossip with each other over MQTT, and
      <a href="post/hacks/awtrix-picow/">an LED matrix running firmware I ported</a> to a board
      nobody had asked it to run on.
    </p>
    <p>
      None of it matters and all of it is fun. Lately I have had an AI assistant at the other
      end of the keyboard, which has made these projects move faster than they have any right
      to. The code is on
      <a href="https://github.com/flavio-fernandes">GitHub</a> &ndash;
      <a href="https://github.com/flavio-fernandes/oclock">oclock</a>,
      <a href="https://github.com/flavio-fernandes/bedclock">bedclock</a>,
      <a href="https://github.com/flavio-fernandes/mqtt2kasa">mqtt2kasa</a> and friends &ndash; and the
      write-ups live under <a href="categories/hacks/">hacks</a>.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>
